## Abstract

This paper analyses the “night effect”, reflecting the introduction of night trading, on intraday trading patterns in the Chinese precious metals futures markets. The main results are summarized as follows: Firstly, both intraday momentum and reversal effect are significant. Secondly, before the launch of night trading, the first half-hour daytime returns have significant predictive power, whereas after the introduction of night trading, the first half-hour night returns become a significant predictor. This change can be attributed to the immediate reactions of domestic investors to international news released in the night session. Thirdly, the driving force of intraday predictability is demonstrated by evidence showing that intraday reversals are mainly driven by liquidity oversupply offered by irrational uninformed traders. Fourthly, the market timing strategy outperforms the always-long and buy-and-hold benchmark strategies. Overall, this study reveals that the introduction of night trading significantly alters intraday return predictability patterns of Chinese precious metals futures markets, benefiting regulators by highlighting the need for enhanced overnight market monitoring.

## Introduction

With the rapid development of high-frequency trading, the intraday return predictability has received much attention. The pioneering work of Gao, Han, Zhengzi Li, and Zhou (2018) extends the time-series momentum to the intraday level, and documents an intraday momentum pattern in the US stock market, that is, the last half-hour return can be positively predicted by the first half-hour return within the same day. Subsequently, this intraday predictive pattern has been examined in several other markets, such as foreign exchange rates (e.g., Elaut, Frömmel, & Lampaert, 2018), US crude oil (e.g., Wen, Wang, & Zhang, 2021), US commodities (e.g., Xu, Bouri, Saeed, & Wen, 2020), Chinese stock markets (e.g., Chu, Gu, & Zhou, 2019; Li, Shen, Wang, & Zhang, 2020; Zhang, Ma, & Zhu, 2019), Asia-Pacific stock markets (Limkriangkrai, Chai, & Zheng, 2023), and VIX futures market (Huang, Tsai, Weng, & Yang, 2023). Notably, other unique intraday predictive patterns have been found in Chinese markets. For example, Chu et al. (2019) and Wen, Gong, Ma, and Xu (2021) show intraday reversals in the Chinese stock market and Chinese crude oil futures market, respectively.

In this paper, we contribute to this line of research by examining the intraday return predictive pattern in the Chinese precious metal futures markets. Our focus is on two main precious metal futures in the Chinese markets, gold and silver. Interest in precious metals futures comes from the heavy trading of these contracts and their roles in asset allocation and hedging possibilities. Firstly, gold is a good representative of precious metals and plays a crucial role in the global financial system, which is often seen as a hedge or safe haven against stock market price declines, economic uncertainty, and inflation (Aye, Chang, & Gupta, 2016; Beckmann, Berger, & Czudaj, 2018; Chen & Wang, 2019). Silver, apart from being a type of precious metal with similar functions to gold in the financial system, also has a wide range of industrial applications such as catalysts, photographic materials, and jewellery production, making the fluctuation in its price a concern for institutional investors (Li, Cheng, & Fang, 2020). Notably, the market of precious metal futures in China has experienced rapid growth over the last few years. According to Jiang, Kellard, and Liu (2020), the Shanghai Futures Exchange (SHFE) ranks second globally in gold trading volume, mirroring China's great importance in global precious metal futures markets. Moreover, China's gold and silver futures markets have strong links to US gold and silver futures markets (Luo & Ye, 2015; Wei, Liang, Li, Zhang, & Wei, 2020). This makes the Chinese gold and silver futures markets ideal test subjects to investigate how cross-market (US) information affects futures intraday return predictability.

Compared to other markets, Chinese gold and silver futures markets have some unique features. SHFE first launched night trading sessions, from 9:00 p.m. to 2:30 a.m. (see Fig. 1), in Chinese gold and silver futures markets in July 2013, which a feature distinguished from other markets. The night trading sessions overlap with the trading periods of other major global metal futures exchanges, such as Commodity Exchange Inc. (COMEX), during which times major market news is often released. This overlap enables Chinese market participants to react to global developments in real time. As shown in Fig. 1, before July 2013, SHFE's trading hours aligned with the evening hours of Eastern Standard Time (EST). However, after the introduction of the night trading session in July 2013, SHFE extended its trading hours to 8:00 PM - 1:30 AM EST, coinciding with the peak period for news releases and heightened market activity in COMEX. Jiang et al. (2020) find that the introduction of night trading sessions has led to enhanced connectivity with global markets and more internalized Chinese markets. However, whether the launch of night trading impacts Chinese gold and silver futures markets at the intraday trading level remains largely unexplored. Secondly, according to the China Futures Market Yearbook (2016), 97.04 % of investors in the commodity futures market are individual investors, whose trading behaviour is very different from that of institutional investors. Thirdly, the intraday trading volume of Chinese gold and silver futures markets shifted from a U-shape to a W-shape (see Fig. 2) after the introduction of night trading, whereas the US stock market intraday volume exhibits a U-shaped pattern. Gao et al. (2018) claim that the high trading volume in the first and last half hour signal the important information content of market opening and close. In contrast, in Chinese precious metal futures markets, trading volumes in the first half hour of both daytime and night trading sessions are much higher than in other periods. Therefore, an interesting question is which first half hour contains more predictive information and is worth more focus from market participants.

There are several notable findings provided by our empirical analysis. Firstly, both intraday momentum and intraday reversal exist in Chinese precious metal futures markets, but the specific patterns are market-dependent. Before the launch of night trading, the first half-hour return in the daytime is the efficient predictor, even though the intraday predictive patterns differ in the gold and silver markets. After the launch of night trading, the first half-hour return of the night session becomes the dominant predictor, whereas the predictive ability of the first half-hour return in the day session disappears, highlighting the significant impact of night trading on Chinese precious metal futures trading. We confirm this finding by an out-of-sample (OOS) analysis.

Secondly, we offer a potential explanation regarding shifts in intraday predictive patterns, related to international news and associated risks. The night trading session generally coincides with the most active trading hours in the US futures market and important news releases in international markets. After the launch of night trading, domestic investors do not need to wait until morning to trade the information, and thus are inclined to immediately adjust to news and risk from global markets in the evening. Thus, the first half hour of night trading becomes more informative and predictive than the first half hour of day trading. If the theoretical mechanism holds true, we would anticipate that higher volatility in the COMEX futures market preceding the SHFE opening is correlated with more indicative information in the initial half-hour returns of SHFE precious metal futures. Our findings indicate that, in both gold and silver futures markets, stronger intraday reversals and momentum patterns manifest within the subset characterized by higher realized volatility and absolute return values in COMEX markets prior to the SHFE night session.

Thirdly, we consider the drivers of intraday predictability. According to Gao et al. (2018), one possible explanation is investors' infrequent trading behaviour. Infrequent traders who absorb a liquidity shock through holding a sub-optimal position tend to rebalance their portfolios in the next active period, resulting in another liquidity shock with the same direction (Bogousslavsky, 2016). If this explanation holds, we would expect intraday momentum to be stronger on days of low liquidity, since liquidity shocks and rebalancing have a greater impact when markets are low on liquidity. Splitting the entire sample into two subsamples by the median of Amihud (2002) illiquidity index, we find consistent results. The second explanation indicates that intraday momentum is explained based on late-informed investors who receive and process information later than early-informed investors. According to Li, Sakkas, and Urquhart (2022), the slow reaction of investors may be caused by the continuous arrival of small amounts of information that do not attract enough attention. Therefore, using the information discreteness measure of Da, Gurun, and Warachka (2014), we find that the intraday momentum is stronger on days with smooth absorption of information.

There are two closely related pieces of literature on intraday momentum in Chinese commodity futures markets. Jin, Kearney, Li, and Yang (2019) document intraday momentum in several Chinese commodity futures markets, but their study is implemented before the introduction of night trading in 2013. Zhang, Wang, and Li (2020) provide evidence of intraday momentum in Chinese commodity futures markets at an aggregate level by constructing an open-interest-weighted index, overlooking the unique characteristics of individual commodity markets. Most existent literature focuses on intraday momentum, but does not much discuss intraday reversals.

To bridge this research gap concerning intraday reversals, we provide a detailed discussion in this paper. Previous studies tend to attribute intraday reversals to overreaction to news and compensation for liquidity provision (e.g., Da, Liu, & Schaumburg, 2014; Fung, Mok, & Lam, 2000; Grant, Wolf, & Yu, 2005). Under these two explanations, reversals are posited as corrections of temporary mispricing stemming from investor overreactions to information or liquidity shortages. Considering that Chinese futures markets are dominated by individual investors, we provide a new possible explanation, that the initial half-hour price movement accurately mirrors overnight information, referring to Kang, Lin, and Xiong (2022). Nonetheless, due to anchoring effects, irrational and uninformed liquidity providers tend to trade counter to preceding price shifts, inducing price pressures which becomes potent enough to generate intraday reversal patterns. Our findings indicate the existence of intraday momentum following intraday reversals in the Chinese gold and silver futures markets, supporting the existence of liquidity oversupply. Furthermore, we devise a trading strategy by initiating a long (short) position when the first half-hour returns are positive (negative) to examine return dynamics throughout a trading day, which supports the liquidity oversupply explanation. Therefore, our investigation into the impact of liquidity on intraday reversals reveals a direct correlation, that is, higher liquidity corresponds to stronger intraday reversals, furnishing additional substantiation for the liquidity oversupply explanation. Finally, we assess the economic value of intraday prediction by employing the efficient predictor as a trading signal for the market timing strategy. The results show that the market timing strategy outperforms the benchmark always-long strategy and the benchmark buy-and-hold strategy since it can generate a larger return and a larger success rate. This finding is valid for both Chinese gold and silver futures markets, confirming the change of intraday predictability patterns in these markets and providing intraday traders with useful practical implications.

This paper makes several significant contributions and provides new insights. Firstly, it shows that intraday reversal occurs before intraday momentum in Chinese gold and silver futures markets, complementing previous studies on intraday momentum. Secondly, this paper focusses on the impacts of night trading sessions from a new perspective, providing in-depth insights into high-frequency traders, whereas most previous studies mainly discuss the impacts on volatility and market quality (e.g., Jiang et al., 2020; Klein & Todorova, 2021). Notably, this paper provides empirical evidence explaining why the introduction of night trading sessions changed intraday predictive patterns in Chinese futures markets. Thirdly, it provides an exhaustive discussion of the drivers of intraday momentum and intraday reversals, by providing empirical evidence of liquidity oversupply from irrational uninformed traders (Kang et al., 2022). This underlines the unique characteristics of the Chinese futures market, characterized by a substantial proportion of retail traders, which, to the best of our knowledge, is new to the intraday prediction literature. Fourthly, this paper offers a trading strategy based on high-frequency data, providing practical implications for intraday trading, while most previous studies are based on low-frequency, daily or monthly, data (Miffre & Rallis, 2007; Szakmary, Shen, & Sharma, 2010).
